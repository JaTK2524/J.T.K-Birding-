Coppersmith Barbet

Quick Facts

  • Scientific name: Psilopogon haemacephalus
  • Family: Megalaimidae
  • Size: 16–18 cm
  • Weight: 30–50 g
  • Wingspan: 28–32 cm
  • Diet: Fruits, berries, figs, and occasionally insects
  • Habitat: Gardens, orchards, woodland, plantations, parks, and urban areas
  • Behaviour: Arboreal and often remains hidden among foliage while feeding and calling
  • Activity: Diurnal
  • Nesting: Excavates a cavity in a tree branch or trunk
  • Breeding Season: Megalaimidae
  • Clutch Size: 2–4 eggs
  • Call: Repeated metallic 'tuk-tuk-tuk' notes resembling a coppersmith at work
  • Lifespan: Several years in the wild
  • Conservation Status: Least Concern
  • Where to Find: Gardens, orchards, parks, plantations, forests, and tree-filled urban areas

About the Bird

The Coppersmith Barbet is a small, colourful green barbet with a red forehead, red throat, and yellow-and-blue facial markings. Its name comes from its repetitive call, which resembles the ringing sound of a coppersmith striking metal. It feeds mainly on fruits and berries and is commonly found in gardens, orchards, wooded parks, and areas with mature fruiting trees.
