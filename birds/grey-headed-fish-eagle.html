Grey-Headed Fish Eagle

Icthyophaga ichthyaetus

The Grey-Headed Fish Eagle is a powerful fish-eating raptor associated with rivers, lakes, reservoirs, wetlands, and other freshwater habitats. It has a distinctive grey head, dark brown body, broad wings, and a strong hooked bill designed for handling slippery prey. Fish form an important part of its diet, although it may also take other aquatic animals and carrion. The eagle often watches from a prominent tree or perch near water before swooping down to seize prey. Its broad wings allow it to soar efficiently over large bodies of water, while its powerful talons are well suited to carrying fish away from the surface.

Family: Accipitridae
