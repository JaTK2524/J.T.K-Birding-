Indian Cormorant

Quick Facts

  • Scientific name: Phalacrocorax fuscicollis
  • Family: Phalacrocoracidae
  • Size: 63–65 cm
  • Weight: 650–1000 g
  • Wingspan: 90–105 cm
  • Diet: Fish, frogs, crustaceans, and other aquatic animals
  • Habitat: Rivers, lakes, reservoirs, ponds, canals, marshes, and wetlands
  • Behaviour: Dives underwater to catch fish and often rests with wings spread to dry them
  • Activity: Diurnal
  • Nesting: Builds a stick nest in trees or shrubs, usually in colonies
  • Breeding Season: Phalacrocoracidae
  • Clutch Size: 3–5 eggs
  • Call: Usually quiet away from colonies; produces grunts and croaks at nesting sites
  • Lifespan: Several years in the wild
  • Conservation Status: Least Concern
  • Where to Find: Rivers, lakes, reservoirs, ponds, canals, and freshwater wetlands

About the Bird

The Indian Cormorant is a dark waterbird with a long neck, slender bill, and distinctive greenish facial skin. It is an excellent swimmer and dives underwater to catch fish and other aquatic prey. Groups often perch together on exposed branches, rocks, or posts after feeding. It is widely distributed across freshwater wetlands and is especially common in southern and central India.
